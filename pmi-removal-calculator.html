Complete 2026 Guide to Removing Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)

For millions of American homeowners who purchased a home with less than a 20% cash down payment, Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) represents one of the most frustrating recurring monthly charges. PMI protects the mortgage lender against default loss—it offers zero financial protection or equity value to you, the homeowner.

Under the federal Homeowners Protection Act of 1998 (HPA), codified at 12 U.S.C. § 4901 et seq., Congress established clear legal thresholds granting conventional mortgage borrowers the absolute legal right to eliminate monthly PMI premiums once their home equity reaches specific benchmark levels.

The Two Federal Milestones: 80% vs. 78% LTV

The Homeowners Protection Act establishes two distinct cancellation mechanisms that govern conventional mortgages backed by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac:

Federal Statutory Cancellation Milestones

$$\text{Borrower-Requested Cancellation (12 U.S.C. § 4902(a))} \implies \mathbf{80\% \text{ Original LTV}}$$

$$\text{Automatic Servicer Termination (12 U.S.C. § 4902(b))} \implies \mathbf{78\% \text{ Original LTV}}$$

$$\text{Midpoint Final Termination (12 U.S.C. § 4902(c))} \implies \mathbf{180\text{th Month (for 30-Yr Fixed)}}$$

1. Borrower-Requested Cancellation at 80% LTV

When your unpaid principal balance reaches 80.0% of the original property value (based on the original sales price or original appraised value, whichever was lower at closing), you have the legal right to submit a written request to your servicer demanding PMI removal.

Lenders are legally obligated to approve your cancellation if you satisfy four statutory conditions:

  1. Good Payment History: No payments 30 days past due in the prior 12 months, and no payments 60 days past due in the prior 24 months.
  2. No Subordinate Liens: The property cannot be encumbered by a second mortgage, home equity loan, or open HELOC.
  3. Value Preservation: The servicer may order a Broker Price Opinion (BPO) or appraisal (typically costing $150–$450 paid by the borrower) to verify that the home's market value has not declined below its original purchase price.
  4. Written Formal Request: The cancellation is not automatic at 80%—you must initiate written notification. Use our pre-filled letter generator above.

2. Automatic Lender Termination at 78% LTV

If you take no action, federal statute requires the loan servicer to automatically terminate PMI on the exact date your principal balance is scheduled to reach 78.0% of the original value based on the original amortization schedule, provided you are current on payments.

Critical Nuance: Automatic termination is tied to the scheduled calendar date. If you make extra principal payments to pay down your loan faster, your servicer is NOT required to automatically cancel at 78% ahead of the original calendar schedule unless you submit a written 80% borrower request!

Fannie Mae & Freddie Mac: Removing PMI via Market Appreciation

What if your home's market value has soared due to rapid regional real estate appreciation or significant home improvements?

Under Fannie Mae Servicing Guide Section B-8.1-04 and Freddie Mac Chapter 8203, you can request PMI cancellation based on the home's Current Appraised Value rather than the original purchase price:

  • Loans Between 2 and 5 Years Old: Your loan-to-value ratio based on a new lender-ordered appraisal must be 75% or less (meaning 25% current equity).
  • Loans Older Than 5 Years: Your loan-to-value ratio based on a new appraisal must be 80% or less (20% current equity).
  • Substantial Documented Home Improvements: If you added square footage, completed a major permitted kitchen remodel, or finished a basement, the 2-year seasoning requirement is completely waived, and you only need an 80% LTV based on current value.

FHA Loans vs. Conventional Loans: The Lifetime MIP Warning

One of the most widespread points of confusion among US homeowners is the difference between PMI on conventional loans and MIP (Mortgage Insurance Premium) on government-backed FHA loans:

  • Conventional PMI: Governed by the Homeowners Protection Act. It cancels permanently once you reach 80% or 78% LTV with zero change to your interest rate.
  • FHA MIP: For any FHA loan originated after June 3, 2013 where the borrower put down less than 10% (LTV > 90%), the annual MIP lasts for the entire 30-year life of the mortgage! It will NEVER automatically drop off. The only way to eliminate FHA MIP is to execute a conventional rate-and-term refinance once your equity crosses 20%.
