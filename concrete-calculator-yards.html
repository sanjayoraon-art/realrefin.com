How Many Yards of Concrete Do I Need? (The Concrete Yardage Formula)

Calculating accurate concrete yardage is the most critical first step of any residential or commercial flatwork project. Running short of concrete midway through a pour creates an unsightly, structurally weak "cold joint," while over-ordering results in costly ready-mix truck waste fees. Using our professional concrete calculator yards tool, you can instantly determine exact volume in cubic yards, cubic feet, and premixed bag equivalents.

📐 The Universal Concrete Yardage Formula

Cubic Yards = [Length (ft) × Width (ft) × (Thickness in inches ÷ 12)] ÷ 27

Because ready-mix batch plants measure concrete in cubic yards (1 yard = 3 feet × 3 feet × 3 feet = 27 cubic feet), you must first calculate your project's volume in cubic feet and divide by 27.

Concrete Yardage Calculator: Slabs vs. Footings vs. Sonotubes

Different structural shapes require distinct geometric formulas to find concrete volume:

1. Rectangular Slabs, Driveways & Sidewalks

For standard rectangles, multiply length by width by depth. If your slab is 4 inches thick, divide 4 by 12 (0.333 ft) before multiplying by the length and width:

  • 10' × 10' Patio @ 4" thick: 10 × 10 × 0.333 = 33.3 ft³ ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards (+10% buffer = 1.36 yards).
  • 12' × 24' Car Pad @ 4" thick: 12 × 24 × 0.333 = 96.0 ft³ ÷ 27 = 3.56 cubic yards (+10% buffer = 3.92 yards).
  • 20' × 20' Garage Slab @ 4" thick: 20 × 20 × 0.333 = 133.3 ft³ ÷ 27 = 4.94 cubic yards (+10% buffer = 5.43 yards).
  • 24' × 36' Workshop Slab @ 6" thick: 24 × 36 × 0.500 = 432.0 ft³ ÷ 27 = 16.00 cubic yards (+10% buffer = 17.60 yards).

2. Round Sonotubes, Pier Footings & Post Holes

For round concrete piers supporting decks, porches, or pole barns, calculate the cylinder volume using Volume = π × r² × depth:

  • Convert tube diameter to radius in feet: Radius = (Diameter in inches ÷ 2) ÷ 12.
  • Multiply 3.14159 × (Radius)² × Depth in feet × Number of Pier Holes.
  • Divide total cubic feet by 27 to obtain total cubic yards.

Ready-Mix Truck vs. Bags: Which Is Cheaper?

Homeowners frequently wonder whether to order a concrete mixer truck or hand-mix bags. Follow this general contractor guideline:

  • Under 1.5 Cubic Yards (Fewer than 70 bags): Buy premixed bags. Delivery trucks charge an expensive "short-load fee" ($75 to $150) if you order less than their 3 to 4 yard minimum delivery capacity. Hand mixing in a wheelbarrow or renting a portable electric mixer from Home Depot ($45/day) is more economical.
  • Over 2.5 Cubic Yards (Over 110 bags): Always order a ready-mix truck. Moving and mixing 110 eighty-pound bags equates to nearly 9,000 pounds of dry material—an exhausting, multi-day job prone to inconsistent water-to-cement ratios. A ready-mix truck discharges your entire yardage in 20 minutes with engineered 3,500–4,000 psi consistency.

A 10 ft by 10 ft slab that is 4 inches thick requires 1.23 cubic yards of concrete without waste (33.3 cubic feet). With a recommended 10% waste buffer, you should order 1.36 cubic yards or purchase 62 bags of 80-pound concrete (or 82 bags of 60-pound concrete).
A standard residential ready-mix concrete truck typically carries a maximum legal load of 9 to 10 cubic yards (weighing approximately 36,000 to 40,000 pounds). Larger commercial mixer trucks can carry up to 11 to 12 cubic yards depending on county road axle-weight limitations.
Soil sub-bases are rarely perfectly level, wooden forms flex outward under the hydrostatic weight of wet concrete, and material clings to tools and pump hoses. If you order only the exact theoretical volume, you risk running out just feet from finishing the slab. A 10% margin guarantees a continuous monolithic pour.
Standard structural concrete weighs approximately 150 pounds per cubic foot, which equals 4,050 pounds (about 2 tons) per cubic yard. Lightweight concrete blends weigh approximately 3,000 pounds per yard.
