🌴 South Carolina (SC) Senior Real Estate Guide

Buying a Retirement Home in South Carolina with a Reverse Mortgage

South Carolina is one of America's premier retirement destinations, attracting thousands of seniors every year to Charleston, Myrtle Beach, Hilton Head Island, Greenville, and Beaufort. The HUD HECM for Purchase program allows seniors relocating to the Palmetto State to buy their dream retirement home using profits from the sale of an up-north home, without triggering new monthly mortgage payments.

⚖️

South Carolina Attorney Closing State Mandate

Under South Carolina Supreme Court precedent and S.C. Code Ann. § 40-5-310, all real estate closings, title certifications, and reverse mortgage settlements must be supervised by an independent, licensed South Carolina attorney. Title companies cannot close loans alone in SC.

🏡

SC Senior Homestead Property Tax Exemption

Once you turn 65 and have been a legal South Carolina resident for at least one full calendar year, the SC Homestead Exemption waives local property taxes on the first $50,000 of fair market value of your legal primary residence.

📜

State Deed Recording Fees (Deed Stamps)

In South Carolina, property conveyances incur a state deed recording fee of $1.85 per $500 ($3.70 per $1,000) of consideration. Our calculator automatically adjusts the closing settlement ledger to account for South Carolina deed recording stamps and attorney representation.

🛡️

FHA Non-Recourse Protection

Whether purchasing in Coastal Carolina or the Upstate, all HECM reverse mortgages are federally insured by FHA. You or your heirs can never owe more than the fair market value of the home when the loan comes due, protecting all other estate assets.

Complete Guide to Reverse Mortgage for Purchase (HECM for Purchase)

The HECM for Purchase (H4P) loan is a specialized program authorized under Section 255 of the National Housing Act by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) and insured by the Federal Housing Administration (FHA). Created under the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 (HERA), this financial mechanism allows homebuyers aged 62 and older to purchase a new primary residence by combining personal cash equity (a down payment) with reverse mortgage proceeds—in a single transaction with zero mandatory monthly mortgage payments.

How the Reverse Mortgage Purchase Down Payment is Calculated

Unlike a conventional 30-year purchase mortgage where the down payment is simply a set percentage (e.g., 5%, 10%, or 20%), a reverse mortgage purchase down payment is calculated through HUD's actuarial Principal Limit Factor (PLF).

Mathematical Model for Reverse Mortgage Purchase Down Payment:
$$\text{Maximum Claim Amount (MCA)} = \min(\text{Purchase Price}, \text{Appraised Value}, \$1,209,750)$$ $$\text{Gross Principal Limit} = \text{MCA} \times \text{PLF}(\text{Age}, \text{EIR})$$ $$\text{Net Loan Proceeds} = \text{Gross Principal Limit} - \sum(\text{Financed Closing Costs} + \text{UFMIP})$$ $$\text{Required Down Payment} = \text{Purchase Price} - \text{Net Loan Proceeds}$$

Where $\text{PLF}(\text{Age}, \text{EIR})$ is the Principal Limit Factor derived from HUD Table 2017-12, determined by the exact age of the youngest borrower (or non-borrowing spouse) and the 10-year Expected Interest Rate (EIR).

1. Age of the Youngest Borrower

HUD requires that the loan calculation be indexed to the age of the youngest borrower on title, or the youngest eligible Non-Borrowing Spouse (NBS). The older the youngest borrower, the higher the Principal Limit Factor (PLF), meaning the FHA allows a larger loan amount. As a result, older seniors require a smaller cash down payment than younger retirees.

2. Expected Interest Rate (EIR)

The Expected Interest Rate is not the note rate used to calculate monthly compounding interest; rather, it is a 10-year index (typically the 10-Year Constant Maturity Treasury or SOFR swap rate) plus the lender's margin. A lower EIR yields a higher borrowing limit, reducing the cash required from the buyer at closing.

3. 2026 HUD Lending Limit ($1,209,750)

Effective January 1, 2026, the FHA national HECM Maximum Claim Amount (MCA) ceiling is $1,209,750. If you purchase a luxury retirement home priced above $1,209,750, you can still execute a HECM for Purchase; however, the reverse mortgage loan amount will only calculate against the $1,209,750 cap, and you must cover the difference in additional cash down payment, or look into a private Jumbo Proprietary Reverse Mortgage.

Eligible Sources for the Reverse Mortgage Down Payment

HUD enforces strict underwriting requirements regarding the origination and seasoning of down payment capital for a HECM for Purchase. Permissible sources include:

  • Proceeds from Sale of Prior Residence: The vast majority of H4P buyers sell an existing home with substantial accumulated equity, allocate approximately 45%–55% of the proceeds to buy their new home, and bank the rest in liquid retirement savings.
  • Personal Cash & Bank Accounts: Checking, savings, money market funds, or CDs seasoned for at least 60 days.
  • Retirement Asset Liquidations: Distributions from 401(k), 403(b), traditional IRA, or Roth IRA accounts.
  • Bona Fide Gift Funds: Immediate family members may gift funds toward the down payment, verified by a standard FHA gift letter and evidence of wire transfer.
⚠️ Strictly Prohibited Down Payment Sources:

Under 24 CFR § 206.44, buyers CANNOT use seller concessions, seller financing/second mortgages, credit card cash advances, personal loans, or builder kickbacks toward the HECM purchase down payment. All down payment equity must be fully documented and free of subordinate liens.

Eligible Property Types for HECM for Purchase

To qualify for an FHA H4P loan, the property must serve as the borrower's permanent primary residence and meet HUD Minimum Property Standards (MPS):

  • Single-Family Residences: Standard detached residential homes.
  • 2-to-4 Unit Properties: Duplexes, triplexes, or fourplexes, provided the borrower occupies at least one unit as their legal primary domicile.
  • FHA-Approved Condominiums: Condominium communities on HUD's approved roster or eligible under Single-Unit Approval (SUA).
  • Manufactured Homes: Must be built after June 15, 1976, affixed to a permanent foundation, possess HUD certification tags, and comply with FHA Title II standards.

South Carolina (SC) Reverse Mortgage Rules & Legal Specifics

Homeowners and retirees purchasing real estate in South Carolina benefit from specific state statutes that protect consumers during reverse mortgage transactions:

  • Mandatory Attorney Representation: South Carolina is strictly an "attorney closing state." Per State v. Buyers Service Co., a licensed South Carolina attorney must manage the title search, prepare legal deeds, review HUD-1/CD settlement paperwork, and preside over loan closing.
  • South Carolina Senior Homestead Exemption: Homeowners aged 65 and older who have held South Carolina legal domicile for at least one year qualify for an exemption that wipes out county property taxes on the initial $50,000 of their home's taxable valuation.
  • Mandatory Pre-Loan Counseling: Prior to signing any binding purchase agreement or incurring loan fees, borrowers must complete a formal counseling session with an independent HUD-approved counseling agency (e.g., GreenPath, Money Management International) and obtain a signed HECM Counseling Certificate (Form HUD-92902).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can I use a reverse mortgage to purchase a new construction home? ▼
Yes! HECM for Purchase is widely used for newly built homes in active-adult and master-planned retirement communities. However, the property must be 100% completed with a formal Certificate of Occupancy (CO) issued by the local municipal building department before the reverse mortgage can close.
What happens to the reverse mortgage when I pass away or move? ▼
When the last surviving borrower or eligible non-borrowing spouse permanently vacates the home (due to passing away or moving into an assisted living facility for more than 12 consecutive months), the loan becomes due and payable. Heirs have up to 12 months to decide whether to sell the home, pay off the loan balance, or refinance into a conventional mortgage. Because HECM is a non-recourse loan, if the home is worth less than the loan balance, FHA insurance covers the difference—heirs never inherit personal debt.
Are there income and credit requirements for a HECM for Purchase? ▼
Yes. Under HUD's Financial Assessment rules, lenders evaluate your credit history, property tax history, and residual income to ensure you have sufficient retirement funds (Social Security, pensions, annuities) to pay ongoing property taxes, homeowner's insurance, and HOA dues. If residual income is tight, the lender may establish a Life Expectancy Set-Aside (LESA) from your loan proceeds to pay property taxes and insurance automatically.
Why is a HECM for Purchase better than paying 100% cash? ▼
Paying 100% all-cash traps all of your wealth inside illiquid real estate equity. If you experience an unexpected medical emergency or market downturn, accessing that equity can be difficult on a fixed retirement income. With a HECM for Purchase, you get the exact same $0 monthly mortgage payment as a cash buyer, but you only commit roughly half the cash, keeping the remaining $200,000–$500,000 liquid in retirement accounts to generate investment income.
Can a non-borrowing spouse under age 62 live in the home? ▼
Yes. Under HUD Eligible Non-Borrowing Spouse (NBS) rules, if one spouse is 62+ and the other is younger (e.g., 58), the couple can still buy the home with a HECM for Purchase. The loan amount will be based on the age of the younger spouse (which slightly lowers the initial PLF), but the non-borrowing spouse is legally protected and can remain in the home for life without making monthly payments even after the older borrower passes away.
