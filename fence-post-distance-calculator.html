Complete Fence Post Distance & Vinyl Fencing Cost Guide (2026)

Installing a beautiful, durable fence requires two critical planning steps: calculating the exact distance between fence posts so your installation looks symmetrical and balanced, and budgeting for vinyl panels, structural posts, gate hardware, and concrete footings. Whether you are building a full perimeter privacy fence or a front-yard decorative picket fence, this guide covers the proven formulas contractors use.

How to Calculate Equal Fence Post Distance Formula

The most common mistake amateur DIY builders make is setting full 8-foot (96-inch) panels across the yard and ending up with an awkward, flimsy 1.5-foot sliver panel against the corner. Professional fence builders use the Equal Panel Spacing Formula:

1. Net Fence Run = Total Length - Total Gate Openings
2. Number of Sections = ⌈Net Fence Run ÷ Nominal Panel Width (8 ft)⌉
3. Exact Equal Spacing = Net Fence Run ÷ Number of Sections

Real-World Example: Suppose you have a 62-foot backyard boundary with a 4-foot walk gate:

  • Net Fence Run = 62 ft - 4 ft gate = 58 feet.
  • Sections = 58 ÷ 8 = 7.25. Round up to 8 sections.
  • Equal Post Distance = 58 ÷ 8 = 7.25 feet (7 feet, 3 inches) on-center.
  • By trimming just 9 inches off each vinyl panel's horizontal rails, all 8 sections are identical, structural wind resistance is increased, and the fence looks completely symmetrical!

How Much Concrete Do I Need for Fence Posts?

Fence stability depends entirely on proper concrete footings. Fences act as gigantic wind sails; inadequate concrete depth causes leaning or snapped posts during severe storms.

  • Hole Diameter: Hole width should always be 3 times the diameter of the post. For a standard 5" × 5" vinyl post, dig a 10" to 12" diameter cylindrical hole.
  • Hole Depth: Minimum depth is 1/3 the length of the post. For a 6-foot fence, dig 24 to 30 inches deep. In freezing climates (Northern US / Canada), holes must extend 6 inches below the municipal frost depth line (often 36" to 42").
  • Bag Count Rule:
    • 24" Deep Hole: 1.5 bags (50-lb fast-setting concrete).
    • 30" Deep Hole: 2.0 bags per post.
    • 36" Deep Gate Post: 2.5 to 3.0 bags (reinforced with internal aluminum post stiffeners).

Frequently Asked Questions

How far apart should fence posts be? +
The standard distance between fence posts is 6 to 8 feet on-center (O.C.). For pre-fabricated vinyl privacy fencing and wood panel kits, exactly 8 feet (96 inches) on-center is the universal manufacturing standard. In high-wind zones or for heavy 8-foot-tall privacy walls, spacing posts 6 feet apart provides superior structural resistance.
How much does vinyl fencing cost per linear foot installed? +
In 2026, professional turnkey installation for 6-foot white vinyl privacy fencing averages between $38 and $58 per linear foot, including posts, panels, gate hardware, concrete footings, and labor. Doing it yourself costs between $22 and $34 per linear foot for materials and rented auger equipment.
How many bags of concrete do I need per fence post? +
Most 4x4 wood posts or 5x5 vinyl posts require 1.5 to 2 bags of 50-lb or 60-lb fast-setting concrete (such as Quikrete) per post hole. For gate posts and end/terminal posts that carry dynamic swinging loads or tension, dig holes 30 to 36 inches deep and use 2.5 to 3 bags of concrete.
How deep should fence post holes be dug? +
A standard rule of thumb is to bury at least one-third (1/3) of the post's total length in the ground. For a standard 6-foot-tall fence using 9-foot posts, holes must be dug 30 to 36 inches deep. In colder climates, post holes must extend 6 inches below the local frost line to prevent winter frost heave.
How do you calculate equal fence post spacing on an uneven run? +
Never install full 8-foot sections and leave a tiny 2-foot sliver at the end. Instead, divide your total run length by 8, round up to the nearest whole section count, and divide the total run by that number. For example, a 60-foot run: 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5. Round up to 8 sections. 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 feet (7 feet, 6 inches) between every single post. All panels are trimmed identically for a balanced, professional look.
Does vinyl fencing last longer than wood fencing? +
Yes. High-grade virgin vinyl (PVC) fencing lasts 25 to 35+ years with virtually zero maintenance, whereas pressure-treated wood fences typically last 12 to 18 years and require regular staining, sealing, and rot repairs. Vinyl is completely immune to termites, wood rot, splintering, and rust.
