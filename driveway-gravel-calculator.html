Complete Driveway Gravel Calculator & Cost Guide (2026)

Building or refreshing a gravel driveway is one of the most cost-effective ways to create durable, all-weather vehicle access to a home, garage, pole barn, or rural property. Our free driveway gravel calculator and gravel driveway cost calculator solves the guesswork when planning aggregate deliveries. However, ordering stone from a local quarry or gravel pit requires precise calculations: ordering too little results in costly extra dump truck delivery fees, while ordering too much leaves an unsightly pile of rock in your yard. This guide explains how our gravel calculator for driveway projects converts cubic yards to tons, factors in compaction, and estimates complete material costs.

How to Use a Gravel Calculator for Driveway Projects (Formula)

Whether you are using this gravel calculator driveway tool for a 20-foot turnaround pad or a quarter-mile rural lane, quarries and aggregate haulers sell stone by the Ton or by the Cubic Yard. To figure out how much stone to order:

1. Volume (Cubic Feet) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × (Depth in inches ÷ 12)
2. Volume (Cubic Yards) = Volume (Cubic Feet) ÷ 27
3. Weight (Tons) = Cubic Yards × Stone Density (approx 1.4) × (1 + Compaction % ÷ 100)

Example: Suppose you have a standard 16-foot wide by 50-foot long suburban driveway (800 sq ft) and want a 3-inch top layer of #57 crushed limestone:

  • Cubic Feet = 50 × 16 × (3 ÷ 12) = 200 cubic feet.
  • Cubic Yards = 200 ÷ 27 = 7.41 cubic yards.
  • Weight = 7.41 yards × 1.4 tons/yd³ × 1.10 (compaction factor) = 11.41 Tons.
  • One standard 14-ton tri-axle dump truck will easily deliver this order in a single trip.

The 3-Layer Rule for Building a Long-Lasting Gravel Driveway

Simply dumping 3 inches of gravel onto bare backyard topsoil will fail within 12 months: vehicle tires will force the stones into the soft mud, creating ruts, potholes, and standing water. Professional excavation contractors install gravel driveways in three structural layers:

  1. Subgrade & Geotextile Fabric: Strip all organic topsoil, excavate down 8 to 10 inches, shape a center crown for water runoff, and roll out a heavy-duty woven geotextile fabric. The fabric separates soil from rock, permanently stopping gravel from sinking.
  2. Sub-Base Layer (#3 or #4 Ballast Stone): Lay 4 to 6 inches of large, baseball-sized angular rock. This layer bridges unstable soil and provides massive drainage channels.
  3. Base Layer (Crusher Run / DGA): Add 3 to 4 inches of dense grade aggregate (gravel mixed with stone dust). Compact thoroughly with a heavy vibrating drum roller to form a solid, unyielding foundation.
  4. Surface Course (#57 Stone): Apply a final 2 to 3-inch layer of clean #57 crushed limestone. Water flows freely through the surface, and angular stones lock together to prevent vehicle spinouts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many tons of gravel do I need for a 100 ft driveway? +
For a standard 12-foot-wide by 100-foot-long driveway (1,200 sq ft) with a standard 3-inch depth of #57 crushed limestone, you need approximately 11.1 cubic yards. At a density of 1.4 tons per yard with a 10% compaction buffer, you will need approximately 17.1 tons of gravel (about 1 to 2 standard tri-axle dump truck loads).
How much does a gravel driveway cost per square foot? +
In 2026, gravel driveway materials typically cost between $0.80 and $1.60 per square foot delivered ($35 to $65 per ton). Turnkey professional installation including site excavation, grading, geotextile fabric, and roller compaction averages between $2.00 and $4.50 per square foot.
How many cubic yards are in a ton of gravel? +
One cubic yard of typical crushed stone or driveway gravel weighs approximately 1.4 tons (2,800 pounds). Therefore, 1 ton of gravel equals approximately 0.71 cubic yards (1 ÷ 1.4). Denser road base with rock dust (crusher run) weighs about 1.5 tons per yard (0.67 yards per ton).
How deep should a gravel driveway be? +
For an existing driveway top-dressing refresh, 1.5 to 2 inches is ideal. For a completely new gravel driveway, the total thickness should be 6 to 10 inches installed in 3 distinct layers: a 4-inch sub-base of #3 large ballast stone, a 3-to-4-inch base layer of compacted crusher run (#411 or road base), and a 2-inch top wear course of #57 crushed limestone or washed pea gravel.
How many tons of gravel fit in a dump truck? +
A standard commercial tri-axle dump truck carries between 14 and 16 tons (10 to 12 cubic yards) of gravel per load. A smaller tandem dump truck carries 10 to 12 tons (7 to 9 cubic yards), while a small single-axle truck holds 5 to 7 tons (4 to 5 cubic yards).
What is the best gravel type for a residential driveway? +
The gold standard for residential driveways is #57 crushed limestone or granite (angular 0.5-to-1-inch stone). Its angular edges interlock firmly when driven over, allowing rainwater to drain through while preventing tire displacement. For the bottom base layer, crusher run (DGA with fines) compacts into a concrete-like sub-base.
