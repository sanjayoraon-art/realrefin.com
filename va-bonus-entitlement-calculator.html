How to Read Your VA Certificate of Eligibility (COE)

2

Complete 2026 Guide to VA Bonus Entitlement (Tier 2 Entitlement)

A frequent misconception among active-duty service members, military veterans, and even seasoned real estate agents is that a VA home loan can only be used once in a lifetime, or that a veteran cannot own two properties simultaneously with zero down payment. Under federal statutory guidelines established by 38 U.S.C. § 3703 and modified by the Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act of 2019, eligible borrowers can access Bonus Entitlement—formally designated by the Department of Veterans Affairs as Tier 2 Entitlement or Secondary Entitlement.

This institutional financial model breaks down the mechanical math, federal thresholds, and loan underwriting standards that govern how remaining secondary entitlement calculates purchasing power when an existing VA loan remains active on another property.

Basic Entitlement vs. Bonus (Tier 2) Entitlement: What Is the Difference?

Every eligible veteran begins with two tiers of loan entitlement:

  • Basic Entitlement (Tier 1): Capped by statute at $36,000. When the VA loan program was originated under the Servicemen's Readjustment Act of 1944, home prices were lower. The $36,000 figure corresponds to a 25% guaranty on a $144,000 home ($144,000 \times 0.25 = $36,000).
  • Bonus Entitlement (Tier 2 / Secondary): Designed to keep pace with escalating home prices, Tier 2 entitlement bridges the gap between $144,000 and the local FHFA Conforming County Loan Limit ($806,495 in standard 2026 counties, or up to $1,209,742 in high-cost metro markets).
Official Statutory Entitlement Formula

$$\text{Maximum County Guaranty} = \text{FHFA County Loan Limit} \times 0.25$$

$$\text{Remaining Bonus Entitlement} = \text{Maximum County Guaranty} - \text{Entitlement Already Charged}$$

$$\text{Maximum \$0-Down Purchase Price} = \text{Remaining Bonus Entitlement} \times 4$$

How to Calculate Required Down Payment When Exceeding Zero-Down Threshold

Private mortgage lenders who originate VA-guaranteed mortgages require that the combination of the VA Guaranty plus the Borrower's Cash Down Payment equals at least 25% of the total purchase price.

When a veteran purchases a property that costs more than their maximum zero-down limit, they are not disqualified. Instead, they simply cover 25% of the excess purchase price as a down payment:

Down Payment Shortfall Formula

$$\text{Guaranty Required by Lender} = \text{New Purchase Price} \times 0.25$$

$$\text{Guaranty Deficit} = \max\left(0, \, \text{Guaranty Required} - \text{Remaining Bonus Entitlement}\right)$$

$$\text{Required Cash Down Payment} = \text{Guaranty Deficit} = 0.25 \times \left(\text{Purchase Price} - \text{Max \$0-Down Price}\right)$$

Realistic Numerical Example

Consider an active-duty Army Major stationed at Fort Carson who bought a home in El Paso for $300,000 using a VA loan. Their Certificate of Eligibility (COE) has $75,000 in entitlement charged ($300,000 \times 25\%$).

Upon receiving Permanent Change of Station (PCS) relocation orders to Colorado Springs, they decide to rent out the El Paso home (cash-flowing $400/month) rather than sell it. In Colorado Springs (standard county conforming limit: $806,495), they want to purchase a new single-family residence for $650,000:

  1. County Guaranty Capacity: $\$806,495 \times 0.25 = \$201,623.75$
  2. Remaining Bonus Entitlement: $\$201,623.75 - \$75,000 = \$126,623.75$
  3. Maximum $0-Down Purchase Price: $\$126,623.75 \times 4 = \$506,495$
  4. Excess Price Over Zero-Down Cap: $\$650,000 - \$506,495 = \$143,505$
  5. Required Down Payment: $\$143,505 \times 0.25 = \mathbf{\$35,876.25}$ (only 5.52% down)

By leveraging their bonus entitlement, the veteran purchases a $650,000 home putting only 5.52% down, secures competitive VA interest rates, and pays $0 in monthly private mortgage insurance (PMI), saving roughly $3,360 annually compared to a conventional 5% down loan.

Can You Have Two Active VA Loans Simultaneously?

Yes. Federal underwriting guidelines explicitly allow a veteran to maintain two active VA loans concurrently under specific conditions:

  • Military PCS Relocation Orders: Active duty personnel re-assigned to a new duty station more than 50 miles away can retain their departure home and buy a new primary residence with remaining bonus entitlement.
  • Departure Residence Rental Conversion: The veteran may lease their first property. Most VA lenders allow 75% of the gross lease agreement rent to offset the existing mortgage payment on the borrower's debt-to-income (DTI) ratio.
  • Primary Residence Intent: The new property purchased with Tier 2 entitlement must be occupied by the veteran (or spouse if active duty deployed) as their primary residence within 60 days of closing.

Restoring Your Entitlement: One-Time Restoration & Refinancing

If you want to clear your entitlement charged on your COE, you have three primary routes:

  1. Sell the Prior Property and Pay Off the VA Loan: When the home is sold and the existing VA mortgage is satisfied in full, your full basic and bonus entitlement is completely restored automatically.
  2. Refinance the VA Loan into a Conventional Mortgage: If you have sufficient equity in your departure home, you can execute a conventional rate-and-term refinance to pay off the VA loan balance. Once paid off, you can submit VA Form 26-1872 (Request for Restoration of Entitlement).
  3. One-Time Restoration of Entitlement: Under 38 U.S.C. § 3702(b), veterans are granted a unique, one-time lifetime exception allowing them to restore their full entitlement even if they retain ownership of the property, provided the underlying VA loan balance has been paid in full (e.g., paid off with cash or an unrelated private loan).

Frequently Asked Questions: VA Bonus Entitlement

Bonus entitlement calculations always use the FHFA Conforming County Loan Limit of the county where the new property being purchased is located. If you move from a low-cost standard county ($806,495 limit) to a designated high-cost county like San Diego ($1,077,550 limit) or Honolulu ($1,149,825 limit), your 25% guaranty ceiling expands significantly, giving you much higher zero-down purchasing power.
If a civilian (non-veteran) assumes your VA loan, your entitlement remains tied up on that property until the loan is paid off in full. However, if an eligible fellow veteran assumes your loan and agrees to execute a formal Substitution of Entitlement, your full entitlement is restored immediately. Under 38 U.S.C. § 3703(a)(1)(C), bonus entitlement can only be utilized on loan amounts exceeding $144,000. For loans of $144,000 or less, only basic entitlement ($36,000) applies. If a veteran's basic entitlement has already been exhausted, they cannot use secondary entitlement for a home priced under $144,000.
For veterans who have previously used their VA loan benefit and are not exempt due to a service-connected disability, the funding fee falls into the "Subsequent Use" schedule: 3.30% for 0% down, 1.50% for 5% to 9.99% down, and 1.25% for 10% or more down. Veterans with a 10%+ VA disability rating pay $0.00 funding fee regardless of how many times they use the benefit.
