Comprehensive Guide to Real Estate Closing Costs: Buyer, Seller & VA Loans

Whether you are purchasing your first home, securing a zero-down VA mortgage, or preparing your net sheet to list a property, closing costs represent the single largest lump-sum expense in a real estate transaction outside of the purchase price itself. This guide breaks down exact statutory formulas, lender guidelines, state-by-state variations, and negotiating strategies.

1. Buyer Closing Cost Calculator: What Charges to Expect

For a home buyer, closing costs typically total between 2% and 5% of the total mortgage loan amount. These fees are itemized on page 2 of your federal Closing Disclosure (CD) issued by your lender at least 3 business days prior to consummation:

  • Loan Origination & Underwriting: Charges assessed by the mortgage lender to evaluate, process, and fund your loan (typically 0.5% to 1.0% of the loan amount).
  • Appraisal and Credit Verification: Independent licensed appraisal ($500 to $850) verifying fair market value, credit report fees ($50 to $120), and flood zone certification.
  • Lender’s Title Insurance Policy: Protects the lender's financial interest against preexisting liens, unrecorded encumbrances, or boundary defects.
  • Prepaid Escrow Reserves: Upfront collection of 12 months of homeowners insurance, 2 to 6 months of county property taxes, and per-diem interest accruing from the closing date through month-end.

Buyer Cash-to-Close Equation

$$\text{Cash to Close} = \text{Down Payment} + \text{Lender Fees} + \text{Title \& Escrow} + \text{Gov Taxes} + \text{Prepaids} - \text{Earnest Deposit} - \text{Seller Credits}$$

2. VA Loan Closing Cost Calculator: Funding Fees & Non-Allowables

VA home loans backed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs offer tremendous advantages—including 0% down payment requirements and the absence of monthly private mortgage insurance (PMI). However, veterans must navigate specialized closing cost rules:

Down Payment Tier First-Time VA Loan Use Subsequent VA Loan Use
0% Down (100% LTV) 2.15% 3.30%
5% to 9.9% Down 1.50% 1.50%
10% or Greater Down 1.25% 1.25%
VA Disability (10%+ rating) 0.00% (100% Exempt) 0.00% (100% Exempt)

VA Disability Exemption (38 U.S.C. § 3729)

Veterans receiving disability compensation for a service-connected disability of 10% or higher, active-duty Purple Heart recipients, and surviving spouses entitled to Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC) are completely exempt from paying the VA funding fee. On a $450,000 home purchase with 0% down, this exemption saves the veteran $9,675 (first use) or $14,850 (subsequent use).

VA Non-Allowable Fees and 4% Concession Cap

Under VA Lender Handbook Chapter 8, lenders cannot charge veterans unbundled attorney fees, document preparation fees, or loan processing fees if a 1% origination fee is already assessed. Furthermore, the seller can contribute up to 4% of the reasonable property value in concessions to pay off buyer debts, buy down interest rates, or cover the VA funding fee.

3. Closing Costs Calculator for Seller: Net Proceeds Breakdown

When selling a home, closing costs typically range from 6% to 10% of the gross sales price. The seller's proceeds statement models several critical deductions:

  • Brokerage Commissions: Following the landmark 2024 NAR antitrust settlement, commissions are unbundled. Sellers negotiate their listing agent’s fee (typically 2% to 3%) and choose whether to offer a buyer agent concession.
  • Owner’s Title Insurance Policy: In many states and counties, local custom dictates that the seller purchases an owner’s title policy to guarantee marketable title to the purchaser.
  • Existing Mortgage & Lien Payoffs: Full satisfaction of the primary mortgage, HELOC balances, and accrued per-diem interest through the payoff wire date.
  • State Documentary Stamps & Transfer Taxes: Statutory deed excise taxes levied on the transfer of real property.

4. Tennessee Closing Cost Calculator Guide (TN Transfer & Note Taxes)

Real estate transactions in the State of Tennessee have specific statutory tax rules codified under Tennessee Code Annotated (T.C.A. § 67-4-409):

Tennessee Mortgage Recordation Tax Mathematical Formula

$$\text{TN Mortgage Tax} = \max\left(0, \frac{\text{Loan Amount} - 2000}{100}\right) \times 0.115$$ Example: On a $427,500 mortgage in Nashville (Davidson County), the taxable indebtedness is $425,500. Dividing by 100 and multiplying by $0.115 yields exactly $489.33 in state recordation tax.

Frequently Asked Questions (Closing Cost FAQs)

How much are closing costs on a VA loan, and what is the VA funding fee? ▼
VA loan closing costs typically range between 1% to 3% of the purchase price plus the one-time mandatory VA Funding Fee. For first-time VA loan borrowers with 0% down, the funding fee is 2.15% (reduced to 1.5% with 5% down and 1.25% with 10% down). For subsequent use, the fee is 3.3% with 0% down. Crucially, veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or higher, active-duty Purple Heart recipients, and qualifying surviving spouses are 100% exempt from the VA funding fee ($0 fee).
What closing costs does a buyer pay when purchasing a home? ▼
A home buyer typically pays between 2% and 5% of the loan amount in closing costs. This includes lender charges (origination, underwriting, appraisal, credit check), title and settlement charges (lender's title insurance policy, escrow closing fee, courier), government recording and mortgage taxes, and prepaid escrow reserves (1 year of homeowners insurance, 2 to 6 months of property taxes, and prepaid daily interest).
What closing costs does a seller pay when selling a home? ▼
Home sellers typically incur total closing deductions between 6% and 10% of the sale price. The largest component is real estate brokerage commissions (historically 5% to 6%, now decoupled following the 2024 NAR settlement). In addition, sellers typically pay owner's title insurance policies (customary in many states), state and county transfer taxes (such as Florida Doc Stamps or Tennessee Realty Transfer Tax), attorney or settlement fees, property tax prorations through the closing date, and any negotiated seller concessions or buyer repair credits.
How do Tennessee (TN) real estate closing costs work? ▼
In Tennessee, real estate closing costs include two statutory state taxes under Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-4-409: (1) The Tennessee State Transfer Tax (Realty Transfer Tax) of $0.37 per $100 ($3.70 per $1,000) of value, customarily paid by the seller; and (2) The Tennessee Mortgage Recordation Tax (Indebtedness Tax) of $0.115 per $100 ($1.15 per $1,000) on mortgage debt exceeding $2,000 (the first $2,000 is legally exempt), customarily paid by the buyer. Tennessee has no personal state income tax on real estate capital gains.
Can closing costs be rolled into a mortgage? ▼
On home purchases, conventional and FHA mortgages generally do not allow regular buyer closing costs to be rolled into the loan balance (the loan-to-value is capped at purchase price). However, the VA funding fee and FHA upfront mortgage insurance (UFMIP) can be 100% financed into the total loan balance. On refinances, almost all closing costs can be rolled into the new mortgage.
What is the difference between a Loan Estimate (LE) and a Closing Disclosure (CD)? ▼
A Loan Estimate (LE) is provided by your lender within 3 business days of submitting a mortgage application, outlining expected interest rates and estimated settlement fees. A Closing Disclosure (CD) is the final, legally binding accounting provided at least 3 business days before closing, detailing exact pennies owed and final cash to close.
