How to Calculate Debt to Income Ratio for Mortgage: 2026 Underwriting Guide

Your **Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio** is arguably the single most critical financial metric examined by mortgage underwriters. While your credit score reflects your payment reliability and your down payment reflects your equity stake, your DTI ratio tells the lender whether you actually have enough monthly cash flow to repay your mortgage.

1. The Mathematical Formulas: Front-End vs. Back-End DTI

Lenders calculate two separate debt ratios:

Front-End DTI Formula (Housing Ratio)

$$\text{Front-End DTI} = \left( \frac{\text{Proposed Housing Expense (PITI)}}{\text{Gross Monthly Income}} \right) \times 100$$

Where $\text{PITI} = \text{Principal} + \text{Interest} + \text{Property Taxes} + \text{Hazard Insurance} + \text{PMI} + \text{HOA Dues}$.

Back-End DTI Formula (Total Debt Ratio)

$$\text{Back-End DTI} = \left( \frac{\text{Proposed Housing Expense} + \text{Monthly Consumer Debts}}{\text{Gross Monthly Income}} \right) \times 100$$

Where consumer debts include car loans, student loans, minimum credit card payments, personal installment loans, and child support.

2. Official DTI Limits by Mortgage Loan Program

Conventional (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac) 28% 36% Up to 45% to 50% via Desktop Underwriter (DU) with high credit or 6+ months reserves.
FHA Loan (HUD 4000.1) 31% 43% Up to 46.9% / 56.9% through automated TOTAL Scorecard approval.

3. Debts Included vs. Debts Excluded from DTI

Borrowers frequently misunderstand which expenses impact their debt-to-income ratio:

  • Debts INCLUDED in DTI: Auto loans/leases, student loans (even if in deferment), minimum required credit card payments, personal loans, timeshare payments, co-signed debts, alimony, and child support.
  • Living Expenses EXCLUDED from DTI: Utilities (electric, gas, water), cell phone bills, auto insurance premiums, health insurance, cable/streaming subscriptions, food/groceries, and 401(k) loan repayments.

4. 5 Proven Strategies to Lower Your DTI Ratio Quickly

  1. Pay Off Small-Balance Revolving Credit: Paying off a $1,500 credit card with a $75 minimum payment eliminates $75 of monthly debt instantly, increasing your mortgage buying power by roughly $12,000 to $15,000.
  2. Recast or Refinance Auto Loans: Extending an auto loan term or paying down principal to lower the monthly payment reduces your back-end DTI immediately.
  3. Switch to an Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) Plan for Student Loans: FHA and Fannie Mae accept your actual contractual IDR payment amount (even if $0/month) rather than the default 1% balance assumption.
  4. Add a Non-Occupant Co-Borrower: Conventional and FHA loans permit adding a parent or relative as a co-borrower to combine gross incomes and pull down the aggregate DTI ratio.
  5. Opt for a Temporary 2-1 Rate Buydown: Utilizing seller credits for a 2-1 Buydown lowers your Year 1 and Year 2 monthly mortgage payment, improving initial affordability.

Frequently Asked Questions: Mortgage Debt to Income Ratio

How to calculate debt to income ratio for mortgage underwriting? +
Divide your total monthly recurring debt payments by your gross monthly income (before tax deductions), then multiply by 100. For example, if your proposed mortgage is $2,000, your auto loan and student debt equal $800, and your gross income is $8,000, your back-end DTI is ($2,000 + $800) ÷ $8,000 = 35.0%.
What is the 28/36 rule in real estate? +
The 28/36 rule is a classic underwriting guideline used by mortgage lenders. It suggests that a household should spend no more than 28% of its gross monthly income on housing expenses (front-end DTI) and no more than 36% on total debt service including housing and consumer loans (back-end DTI).
Can I get a mortgage with a 50% DTI ratio? +
Yes. Both Fannie Mae Desktop Underwriter (DU) for Conventional loans and FHA TOTAL Scorecard frequently issue automated approvals for borrowers with back-end DTIs up to 50% (and up to 56.9% for FHA), provided you have strong compensating factors such as a high credit score (740+), substantial cash reserves, or significant residual income.
How are deferred student loans calculated in mortgage DTI? +
Underwriters cannot ignore deferred student loans. For Conventional loans, Fannie Mae allows lenders to use the documented monthly payment on an Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) plan (even if $0). If no payment is documented, the lender must use 0.5% or 1% of the total loan balance as a placeholder monthly debt obligation.
Does rent count toward my DTI ratio? +
No. When buying a home, your current rent payment is completely replaced by your proposed mortgage payment (PITI). Your current rent is never added as a debt on top of your new mortgage.
