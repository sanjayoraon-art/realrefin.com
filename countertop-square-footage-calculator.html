How is Countertop Square Footage Calculated? (Step-by-Step)

Calculating kitchen and bathroom countertop square footage is straightforward once you break your kitchen layout into individual, non-overlapping rectangles. Because countertops are manufactured and sold by the square foot, having an exact measurement protects you from overpaying for raw stone slabs or unexpected contractor change orders.

Square Feet = (Length in Inches × Depth in Inches) ÷ 144

Dividing square inches by 144 converts the measurement into square feet, because 1 square foot contains 144 square inches (12" × 12").

1. Standard Countertop Depth Benchmarks

Most residential base kitchen cabinets are built to an industry standard of 24 inches deep. Countertops are fabricated with a 1.5-inch overhang past the front face of the cabinet frames to prevent spills from dripping directly into drawer slides and cabinet doors.

  • Standard Perimeter Countertops: 25.5 inches deep (24" cabinet + 1.5" front overhang).
  • Bathroom Vanity Tops: 22.5 inches deep (21" vanity cabinet + 1.5" overhang).
  • Kitchen Island with Seating Bar: 36 to 48 inches deep (allows 12 to 15 inches of knee clearance).
  • Peninsula Countertops: 30 to 36 inches deep.

2. Measuring L-Shaped and U-Shaped Corners Correctly

The most common homeowner mistake when measuring L-shaped and U-shaped kitchens is double counting the corners. If you measure both walls all the way into the corner, you will calculate the 25.5" × 25.5" corner square (approx. 4.51 sq ft) twice.

  1. Section A (Long Run): Measure the entire length along the wall all the way into the corner (e.g., 120 inches). Multiply: 120" × 25.5" ÷ 144 = 21.25 sq ft.
  2. Section B (Perpendicular Run): Measure from the edge of Section A outward to the end of the second run (e.g., 74.5 inches). Multiply: 74.5" × 25.5" ÷ 144 = 13.19 sq ft.
  3. Total Net Area: 21.25 + 13.19 = 34.44 sq ft.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do you calculate square footage of a countertop? +
Measure each section's length and width in inches, multiply them together to get square inches, and divide by 144. For example, a 10-foot counter with standard 25.5-inch depth equals (120 × 25.5) / 144 = 21.25 square feet. For complex layouts, divide into non-overlapping rectangles and sum the totals.
Do fabricators deduct sink and cooktop cutouts? +
No. Fabricators must purchase whole stone slabs from the distributor. Because the cutout section cannot be resold, fabricators bill for the full gross slab footage. In addition, undermount sink cutouts require edge profiling and polishing, which usually incurs a $150 to $300 specialty fabrication charge.
How many stone slabs do I need for my kitchen? +
A standard granite slab yields approximately 45 sq ft of usable stone, while a jumbo quartz slab yields 55 to 63 sq ft. For an average 40 sq ft kitchen with an L-shape, 1 jumbo slab or 2 standard slabs are required to accommodate seam placement and grain matching.
Should I choose a 4-inch stone backsplash or full-height tile? +
A 4-inch matching stone riser protects drywall from water and is inexpensive ($10-$15 per linear foot). However, modern kitchen remodels frequently eliminate the 4-inch curb in favor of full-height ceramic/subway tile or a seamless slab backsplash extending all the way to the upper cabinets for an open, luxurious look.
