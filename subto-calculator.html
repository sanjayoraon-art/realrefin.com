Complete 2026 Guide to Subject-To (SubTo) Real Estate Investing

In the real estate investing community, Subject-To (SubTo) financing has emerged as one of the most powerful wealth-generation models of the 21st century. The concept is straightforward yet profound: an investor purchases real property by taking ownership of the deed "subject to" the seller’s existing mortgage staying intact.

Rather than qualifying for a new 7.0%+ bank loan requiring 20% cash down, debt-to-income (DTI) scrutiny, and weeks of underwriting delays, the investor takes over the seller's existing low-rate mortgage (typically originated between 2020 and 2022 at 2.5% to 3.75% fixed interest).

The Mathematical Advantage: Rate Arbitrage in Action

Consider the mathematical difference between purchasing a $400,000 single-family rental with a new bank mortgage versus taking over an existing 3.0% SubTo loan:

Debt Service Comparison Math

$$\text{New Bank Loan: } \$320,000 \text{ at } 7.0\% \implies \text{Monthly P\&I} = \mathbf{\$2,129 / \text{month}}$$

$$\text{SubTo Loan: } \$320,000 \text{ at } 3.0\% \implies \text{Monthly P\&I} = \mathbf{\$1,349 / \text{month}}$$

$$\text{Monthly Debt Service Savings} = \$2,129 - \$1,349 = \mathbf{+\$780 / \text{month (\$9,360 / year!)}}$$

That $780/month difference represents pure passive cash flow that would otherwise have been paid to a bank in interest expense. Over a 5-year holding period, the investor pockets an additional $46,800 in cash flow purely through interest rate arbitrage.

Analyzing the Entry Fee: How to Structure a Winning SubTo Offer

A frequent misconception is that SubTo deals require zero cash. In reality, investors must calculate their Total Entry Fee:

  • Cash to Seller: In some distress situations, sellers walk away with $0; in other deals, sellers negotiate $10,000 to $25,000 in equity buyout.
  • Arrears Reinstatement: Many SubTo sellers are in pre-foreclosure. Reinstating the loan requires bringing the mortgage current by paying missed payments, late fees, and legal fees directly to the bank.
  • Assignment Fee: If a wholesale dispo agent sourced the deal, their fee ($5k–$15k) is factored into the capital stack.
  • Closing & Turnover: Title escrow, recording taxes, Land Trust creation, and minor cosmetic property turnover.
SubTo Cash-on-Cash Return Formula

$$\text{Cash-on-Cash Return (CoC)} = \frac{\text{Net Annual Cash Flow}}{\text{Total Entry Fee}} \times 100$$

$$\text{Payback Velocity (Months)} = \frac{\text{Total Entry Fee}}{\text{Net Monthly Cash Flow}}$$

4 High-Converting SubTo Exit Strategies

  1. Long-Term Rental (LTR): Lease the home to a vetted long-term tenant. The rental income pays off your underlying PITI debt service, covers property management and maintenance reserves, and delivers stable passive monthly cash flow.
  2. Mid-Term Rental (MTR): Furnish the property and rent to traveling healthcare professionals, insurance-displaced families, or corporate relocations. MTR gross rents typically command 1.5x to 2x traditional long-term market rates.
  3. Wraparound Note (The "Wrap"): Sell the property to a deserving buyer who cannot qualify for a bank loan (e.g. self-employed business owners). Collect a 10% down payment from the buyer (which immediately reimburses your entry fee!) and write a wrap note at 8.0%–8.5% interest, pocketing a monthly interest spread with zero maintenance headaches.
  4. Lease Option (Rent-to-Own): Collect an upfront non-refundable option fee ($10,000–$25,000) from a tenant-buyer, plus a monthly rent premium above market rate, giving them the option to purchase the property at a fixed future price.

The Due-on-Sale Clause: Risk Reality vs. Myth

Under paragraph 18 of standard Fannie Mae deeds of trust, banks retain the contractual option to accelerate a loan upon title transfer. However, banks are in the business of collecting interest, not managing foreclosed inventory.

Lenders virtually never call a loan due if three conditions are met:

  • Automated Servicing: Payments are made automatically on the 1st of every month through a licensed servicing company like FCI or Weststar.
  • Proper Insurance Structuring: The hazard policy lists the seller as the Named Insured, the investor's entity or Land Trust as the Additional Insured, and the underlying lender as the Loss Payee.
  • Land Trust Structuring: Under 12 U.S.C. § 1701j-3(d)(8) (Garn-St. Germain Act), transfers into inter vivos trusts where the borrower remains a beneficiary do not trigger the Due-on-Sale clause.
