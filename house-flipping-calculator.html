House Flipping Calculator: The Complete Fix and Flip Deal Analysis Guide

Fix and flip real estate investing is one of the most lucrative wealth-building vehicles when executed with rigorous mathematical underwriting. Unlike passive buy-and-hold investing, flipping houses requires managing complex, simultaneous variables: acquisition discounting, construction scope creep, expensive short-term financing, carrying costs, and exit commissions. This guide details how professional flippers model deals and safeguard their profit margins.

1. The 70% Rule in House Flipping (Maximum Allowable Offer)

The 70% rule is an established industry heuristic used to quickly evaluate whether a distressed residential property is priced low enough to generate a safe profit margin:

The 70% Rule Formula

$$\text{Maximum Allowable Offer (MAO)} = (\text{ARV} \times 0.70) - \text{Estimated Rehab Budget}$$ Example: If a renovated home sells for $370,000 (ARV) and requires $45,000 in repairs, the 70% benchmark dictates you should offer no more than ($370,000 × 0.70) - $45,000 = $214,000. The remaining 30% ($111,000) absorbs carrying costs, hard money interest, agent fees, and net investor profit.

2. Fix and Flip Loan Calculator: Hard Money Debt Modeling

Most house flippers leverage short-term asset-based debt known as hard money loans or bridge financing rather than tying up 100% of their own liquid capital. Key lending parameters modeled in our calculator include:

Rehab Financing Up to 100% of Construction Held in escrow by lender and released in inspection draws.
Interest-Only Rate 9.5% to 13.0% Annual Monthly carrying cost owed regardless of construction speed.

3. Net Profit and Cash-on-Cash Return on Investment Formulas

A high nominal dollar profit can be misleading if the project ties up excessive personal capital for an extended timeline. Professional underwriters evaluate both absolute dollar profit and Cash-on-Cash Return on Investment (CoC ROI):

Net Profit and Annualized ROI Equations

$$\text{Net Profit} = \text{ARV} - (\text{Purchase} + \text{Buying Costs} + \text{Rehab} + \text{Financing} + \text{Holding} + \text{Resale Costs})$$ $$\text{Cash-on-Cash ROI} = \frac{\text{Net Pre-Tax Profit}}{\text{Total Out-of-Pocket Cash Invested}} \times 100$$ $$\text{Annualized ROI} = \text{Cash-on-Cash ROI} \times \left(\frac{12}{\text{Holding Months}}\right)$$

Frequently Asked Questions (House Flipping FAQs)

How do you calculate profit on a house flip? ▼
House flipping profit is calculated by subtracting all project expenditures from the final resale price (After Repair Value or ARV). The formula is: Net Profit = ARV - Purchase Price - Buying Closing Costs - Rehab Costs - Holding/Carrying Costs - Hard Money Loan Fees & Interest - Resale Realtor Commissions & Closing Costs.
What is the 70% rule in house flipping? ▼
The 70% rule is a foundational real estate investing guideline used to calculate the Maximum Allowable Offer (MAO). It states that an investor should pay no more than 70% of the property's estimated After Repair Value (ARV), minus the estimated cost of repairs: MAO = (ARV × 0.70) - Estimated Rehab Costs. The remaining 30% accounts for holding expenses, financing costs, closing fees, and developer profit.
What is a good ROI for a fix and flip deal? ▼
Most professional house flippers target a minimum net profit margin of 15% of the ARV or at least $30,000 to $50,000 in absolute dollar profit to account for unforeseen construction delays. In terms of cash-on-cash return on invested capital, experienced investors seek an annualized ROI of 20% to 35% or higher.
How does a fix and flip loan calculator work? ▼
A fix and flip loan calculator models short-term asset-based debt (hard money or private bridge loans). Typically, hard money lenders finance 75% to 90% of the purchase price and up to 100% of the renovation budget at interest-only rates between 9.5% and 13.0%, along with 1 to 3 upfront origination points. The calculator computes upfront equity requirements and monthly carrying interest through project completion.
What carrying costs are involved during a house flip? ▼
Carrying or holding costs include property taxes prorated for the holding duration (typically 4 to 6 months), builder's risk insurance, utilities (electric, gas, water for contractor crews), HOA fees, lawn maintenance, and municipal permit fees.
