How to Forecast Short-Term Rental Revenue (Without Expensive Subscriptions)

Predicting cash flow for an Airbnb or VRBO vacation rental requires transitioning beyond basic long-term rental heuristics. While conventional multifamily investing calculates income based on a fixed 12-month lease rate, short-term rentals (STR) function as active hospitality micro-businesses governed by Average Daily Rate (ADR), dynamic seasonality, local regulation, and turnover frequency.

Rather than relying strictly on paid enterprise data platforms like AirDNA, experienced operators build granular pro-forma spreadsheets evaluating local hotel RevPAR data, active competitor calendars, and strict cleaning fee pass-through margins.

The Core Short-Term Rental Net Operating Formula
$$\text{Net Monthly Profit} = (\text{ADR} \times \text{Booked Nights}) + \text{Cleaning Spread} - \text{Fixed Housing} - \text{Operating Costs} - \text{Platform Fees}$$

Understanding Key Industry Metrics: ADR vs. RevPAR

When underwriting an investment property, two core acronyms dictate deal viability:

  • Average Daily Rate (ADR): The average dollar amount collected for each paid booked night: $$\text{ADR} = \frac{\text{Total Nightly Accommodation Revenue}}{\text{Number of Paid Booked Nights}}$$
  • Revenue Per Available Room (RevPAR): The gold-standard hospitality metric that integrates your occupancy rate to reveal true revenue efficiency across all 30 calendar nights: $$\text{RevPAR} = \text{ADR} \times \text{Occupancy Rate} = \frac{\text{Gross Nightly Revenue}}{\text{Total Calendar Nights Available}}$$

💡 Pro Host Strategy: The Cleaning Fee Spread Arbitrage

Novice hosts treat cleaning fees as a pure reimbursement pass-through. Experienced short-term rental operators structure their cleaning fee slightly above direct contractor costs (e.g., charging $140 while paying cleaning crews $115 per turnover). With an average length of stay of 3.2 nights, a property experiences 7 to 9 turnovers per month—generating \$175 to \$225 in extra recurring monthly margin that shields against minor linen wear-and-tear.

Frequently Asked Questions About Airbnb Financial Modeling

How do you calculate net profit from an Airbnb rental? +
Net Airbnb profit is calculated by taking Gross Rental Revenue (Average Daily Rate × Booked Nights) + Cleaning Fees Collected, and subtracting all operating deductions: Airbnb host platform fees (typically 3%), cleaning expenses paid, utilities (electric, water, gas, high-speed WiFi), consumable guest supplies, STR insurance, local transient occupancy taxes (TOT), and fixed housing debt service (mortgage PITI or master lease rent).
What is a good profit margin for an Airbnb or Short-Term Rental (STR)? +
For purchased properties, a healthy net Airbnb operating margin ranges between 20% and 35% of gross revenue, yielding a Cash-on-Cash return of 15% to 25%+. For Airbnb rental arbitrage (leasing a property and subleasing on Airbnb with landlord approval), successful operators aim for at least $1,000 to $1,500 net monthly profit per unit after paying master lease rent and utilities.
What is the difference between ADR and RevPAR in short-term rentals? +
Average Daily Rate (ADR) measures the average rental revenue paid per paid occupied night: Total Nightly Revenue ÷ Booked Nights. In contrast, Revenue Per Available Room (RevPAR) reflects overall asset monetization efficiency by dividing Total Nightly Revenue by Total Available Calendar Nights (or ADR × Occupancy Rate). A property with a $200 ADR at 70% occupancy has a RevPAR of $140.
What are typical Airbnb host service fees in 2026? +
Under Airbnb's standard "Split-Fee" structure, the host pays approximately 3% of the booking subtotal (nightly rate + cleaning fee), while the guest pays a 14% to 16% service fee. Under the "Host-Only Fee" structure (mandatory for software-connected professional property managers and hospitality brands), the host pays the entire 14% to 16% service fee, with zero fee charged to the guest.
How does cleaning fee pass-through affect short-term rental profitability? +
If a host charges guests $150 per turnover and pays cleaning crews $120, the $30 spread generates auxiliary net revenue ($30 × 5 turns = $150/month). Conversely, undercharging for cleanings turns housekeeping into an unbudgeted operational loss. In our calculator, you can model cleaning fees collected versus cleaning costs paid directly.
