Understanding Hard Money Loans & Private Bridge Lending

A hard money loan is a specialized, short-term debt instrument secured by real estate collateral and underwritten by private lending institutions or high-net-worth individual investors. Unlike conventional commercial mortgages that prioritize personal debt-to-income (DTI) metrics and tax returns, hard money lenders make credit approvals based on Loan-to-Cost (LTC) and the After Repair Value (ARV) of the property.

Real estate fix-and-flippers, residential redevelopers, and commercial operators rely on hard money loans when velocity of execution is paramount—typically closing deals in 5 to 10 business days where traditional banks require 45 to 60 days of bureaucratic committee underwriting.

The Hard Money Monthly Payment Formula
$$\text{Monthly Payment (Interest-Only)} = \frac{\text{Total Loan Balance} \times \text{Annual Interest Rate}}{12}$$

How Origination Points & Fees Impact Your Upfront Cash to Close

Hard money financing entails two distinct cost categories: upfront fees and monthly carrying interest.

  • Origination Points: Lenders charge between 1.5 and 3.5 points at closing. One point represents 1.0% of the gross loan facility. On a \$200,000 total loan, 2.5 points equates to \$5,000 deducted directly from loan proceeds or added to cash required at closing.
  • Underwriting & Document Preparation: Private lenders incur legal review, appraisal inspection, and flood certification costs, routinely itemized as a flat fee between \$1,200 and \$2,000.
  • Rehab Draw Inspection Fees: When you request milestone reimbursements from the construction escrow, the lender dispatches a third-party inspector (typically \$200 to \$350 per draw request).

⚠️ Crucial Investor Insight: Dutch vs. Non-Dutch Interest

In a Dutch Interest arrangement, the lender assesses interest on your total loan commitment from Day 1—meaning you pay interest on your \$50,000 renovation escrow even while the money sits untouched in the bank during the first 3 months. In a Non-Dutch structure, you only pay interest on funds after they are physically disbursed. Over a 9-month project, Dutch interest can add \$2,500 to \$6,000 in phantom financing carry costs!

Frequently Asked Questions About Hard Money Loans

What is a hard money loan and how does it work? +
A hard money loan is a short-term, asset-based bridge financing instrument funded by private lenders rather than conventional commercial banks. Eligibility is anchored to the property's collateral equity and After Repair Value (ARV) rather than personal W-2 income or debt-to-income (DTI) ratios. Hard money loans typically feature terms from 6 to 24 months, interest-only monthly payments, and balloon principal payoff upon property sale or refinance.
How is the monthly payment on a hard money loan calculated? +
Because virtually all hard money loans are structured as interest-only debt without principal amortization, the monthly payment is computed using the formula: Monthly Payment = (Total Loan Balance × Annual Interest Rate) ÷ 12. For example, a $200,000 hard money loan at 12.0% interest yields a monthly debt service payment of ($200,000 × 0.12) ÷ 12 = $2,000.00.
What are origination points and how much do hard money lenders charge? +
Origination points are upfront fees paid to the lender at closing, where one point equals 1.0% of the total loan commitment amount. Hard money lenders typically assess between 1.5 and 3.5 points depending on borrower track record, deal profitability, and loan-to-cost (LTC). For a $250,000 loan, 2.0 points equals a $5,000 upfront fee deducted at closing.
What is the difference between Dutch Interest and Non-Dutch Interest? +
In a "Dutch Interest" structure, the borrower pays monthly interest on the entire approved loan balance from day one—including renovation funds held back in the construction escrow. In a "Non-Dutch" structure, the borrower pays interest strictly on the initial purchase funds plus renovation funds as they are drawn in phases. Dutch interest increases total financing carry costs by 15% to 25%.
What are typical Loan-to-Cost (LTC) and Loan-to-ARV (LTARV) limits? +
Hard money lenders typically advance up to 85% to 90% of the purchase price and 100% of the renovation budget, subject to a strict ceiling of 70% to 75% of the appraised After Repair Value (ARV). Whichever threshold produces the lower loan amount governs the maximum advance.
Can you refinance out of a hard money loan? +
Yes. While fix-and-flippers retire hard money loans through the retail resale of the property, buy-and-hold BRRRR investors refinance the short-term debt into long-term 30-year fixed DSCR or conventional mortgages once renovations are complete and the asset is stabilized with a paying tenant.
