How to Calculate How Many Boxes of Flooring You Need

Whether you are installing laminate flooring, luxury vinyl plank (LVP), or engineered wood, purchasing the exact amount of flooring requires three steps: finding the net room square footage, adding the proper cut waste factor, and dividing by the square feet per carton.

Total Boxes to Buy = CEILING( (Net Square Footage × (1 + Waste Factor)) ÷ Sq Ft Per Box )

Always round UP to the nearest whole integer. Flooring cannot be purchased in fractional cartons.

Why is a 10% Waste Factor Required?

Plank flooring manufacturers require end joints to be staggered by at least 6 to 8 inches from adjacent rows. Cutting planks to fit against walls, heating vents, doorways, and closet corners creates scrap cutoffs that cannot always be reused.

  • 10% Waste: Standard rectangular bedrooms, living rooms, and open basements with straight horizontal or vertical plank layout.
  • 15% Waste: Rooms with 45-degree angled walls, multiple continuous doorways, hallway intersections, or herringbone patterns.
  • 20% Waste: Custom diagonal or intricate border designs with high scrap loss.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How many extra boxes of flooring should I keep for attic stock? +
Always retain at least 1 unopened box (approximately 20 to 25 sq ft) of flooring as "attic stock." Flooring dye lots, plank locking systems, and manufacturer product lines change rapidly. If a plank gets damaged by a water leak, dropped pot, or heavy furniture five years from now, having identical factory replacement planks saves you from having to replace the entire room.
Do I need to acclimate laminate and LVP flooring before installation? +
Yes. Laminate flooring contains a high-density wood fiberboard core (HDF) and must acclimate in the unopened cartons laid flat in the room for at least 48 hours at normal living temperature (65°F to 75°F). Rigid core SPC vinyl plank is less temperature sensitive, but manufacturers still recommend 24 to 48 hours of acclimation to prevent plank buckling or joint separation.
What transition strips do I need for doorways? +
For doorways between rooms with identical floor heights, use a T-Molding. When transitioning down to low pile carpet, vinyl, or concrete, use a Reducer Strip. At exterior doors, sliding glass doors, or against vertical brick fireplaces, use an End Cap (Baby Threshold). At stairs, use a flush or overlap Stair Nose molding.
