Deck Stairs Cost Calculator: IRC Code, Stringers & Rise/Run

Building deck stairs requires strict adherence to International Residential Code (IRC) standards. A single code violation (such as uneven riser heights or bouncy stringers) will fail building inspection and present severe trip hazards:

  • Maximum Step Rise: 7.75 inches (Ideal: 7.0 to 7.25 inches).
  • Minimum Step Run (Tread Depth): 10 inches (Ideal: 11 to 11.25 inches to accommodate two standard 5.5" deck boards with a 1/4" overhang).
  • Stringer Spacing Rule for Composite: While pressure-treated wood lumber can span stringers spaced 16 inches on center, composite and PVC decking (TimberTech, Trex, Fiberon) requires stringers spaced at a maximum of 12 inches on center (or 9-10 inches for commercial/diagonal treads) to eliminate spongy deflection.
  • Concrete Footer Landing Pad: The bottom of the stair stringers must rest on a solid, frost-proof concrete landing pad (minimum 4 inches thick) rather than direct dirt or grass.

Deck Stairs Rise & Stringer Calculation Formula

1. Number of Risers = Round(Total Vertical Rise ÷ 7.25 inches)
2. Exact Riser Height = Total Vertical Rise ÷ Number of Risers
3. Number of Treads = Number of Risers - 1
4. Total Horizontal Run = Number of Treads × 11 inches
5. Stringers Required = ⌈ Stair Width (inches) ÷ 12 ⌉ + 1 (for 12" O.C. composite)

How to Estimate Total Paintable Surface Area

Homeowners frequently underestimate paint requirements by measuring only the flat floorboards. To get the true paintable area:

  • Floor Area: Length × Width (e.g., 12' × 16' = 192 sq ft).
  • Railing Surface Area: Multiply railing linear feet by 2.5 (e.g., 40 linear feet × 2.5 = 100 paintable sq ft for balusters, top rails, and bottom plates).
  • Stairs Surface Area: Multiply the number of steps by step width by 2.5 (to account for treads, vertical risers, and cut stringer faces).

Frequently Asked Questions About Decks, Stairs & Maintenance

How much does a TimberTech deck cost per square foot in 2026? +
In 2026, the turnkey cost to have a licensed contractor build a complete TimberTech composite deck (including pressure-treated substructure framing, footings, boards, fasteners, and aluminum/composite railings) ranges from $35.00 to $65.00+ per square foot. Materials alone cost $14.00 to $26.00 per sq ft depending on the collection: TimberTech Edge ($6.50–$8.50/sq ft board only, $35–$45/sq ft turnkey); TimberTech PRO ($9.50–$13.50/sq ft board only, $44–$58/sq ft turnkey); and TimberTech AZEK Advanced PVC ($14.00–$18.50/sq ft board only, $55–$72/sq ft turnkey).
How much does it cost to build deck stairs? +
Professionally built deck stairs typically cost between $1,200 and $3,500 for a standard 4-to-8 step flight, or approximately $45.00 to $95.00 per step installed. Key cost factors include the total vertical rise, number of pressure-treated 2x12 cut stringers (composite decking requires 12-inch stringer spacing rather than 16-inch to prevent tread sagging), composite riser fascia trims, handrail installation, and a poured concrete landing pad at the base.
How much does it cost to paint or stain a deck per square foot? +
Professionally painting or staining an existing wood deck in 2026 costs between $2.00 and $4.50 per square foot, including pressure washing, chemical deck cleaner/brightener, rough sanding, minor board repairs, and applying two coats of solid acrylic deck stain or paint. On a standard 300 square foot deck with railings and stairs, professional painting costs $950 to $1,800. Doing it DIY typically costs $180 to $380 for 3 to 5 gallons of quality stain, rollers, and prep chemicals.
What is the difference between TimberTech Edge, PRO, and AZEK PVC? +
TimberTech Edge features a 3-sided capped composite core with scalloped underside, offering an affordable entry-point with a 25-year warranty. TimberTech PRO is a 4-sided capped composite board with zero exposed wood core on top, bottom, or grooves, featuring superior mold resistance and a 30-year fade/stain warranty. TimberTech AZEK is engineered from 100% advanced cellular PVC with zero organic wood fibers, offering unmatched 50-year warranty, staying up to 30 degrees cooler under bare feet, and meeting Class A flame spread ratings.
How many gallons of paint or stain do I need for my deck? +
One gallon of quality deck stain or paint typically covers 200 to 250 square feet for a single coat on smooth wood, or 150 to 180 square feet per coat on weathered, porous cedar or pressure-treated pine. Because outdoor decks require two coats for durability and UV protection, a 300 sq ft deck floor requires approximately 3 to 4 gallons of stain. If you also have railings and stairs, plan on an additional 2 to 3 gallons.
Why do composite deck stairs require stringers spaced 12 inches on center? +
Unlike solid wood lumber which has high natural rigidity, composite and PVC deck boards are more flexible under point loads. When people walk up or down stairs, their full body weight impacts the center of the tread. International Residential Code (IRC) and manufacturers like TimberTech and Trex require stringers to be spaced at a maximum of 12 inches on center (or 9-10 inches for commercial) to prevent tread bounce, flexing, and cracking.
