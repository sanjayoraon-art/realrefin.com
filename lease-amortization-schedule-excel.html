How to Create a Lease Amortization Schedule in Excel (Formula Guide)

Whether you are an accountant complying with FASB ASC 842, an auditor verifying IFRS 16 standards, or a commercial real estate tenant modeling equipment or property rent, here is the complete step-by-step formula blueprint to construct a lease amortization schedule in Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets.

1 Set Up Your Lease Assumptions Input Block

In a new Excel worksheet, set up your inputs in cells A1:B6:

  • Cell B1 (Monthly Lease Payment): e.g., 3500
  • Cell B2 (Annual Discount Rate / IBR): e.g., 6.5%
  • Cell B3 (Lease Term in Months): e.g., 60
  • Cell B4 (Payment Timing Type): Enter 1 for payments in advance (Beginning of Month) or 0 for arrears (End of Month).
  • Cell B5 (Guaranteed Residual Value): e.g., 0 (or salvage value/purchase option).
2 Calculate Initial Lease Liability with Excel's =PV() Function

Under ASC 842, the Day 1 lease liability equals the present value of all remaining lease payments discounted at the tenant's incremental borrowing rate (IBR). Use this exact Excel formula:

# Excel Syntax: =PV(rate / 12, nper, -pmt, -[fv], [type]) =PV(B2/12, B3, -B1, -B5, B4)

Mathematical representation:

$$\text{Present Value (PV)} = \sum_{t=0}^{n-1} \frac{\text{Payment}_t}{\left(1 + \frac{r}{12}\right)^t} + \frac{\text{Residual Value}}{\left(1 + \frac{r}{12}\right)^n}$$
3 Construct the 6 Schedule Columns

Starting in row 9, label the following columns:

  • Column A (Period / Month): 0, 1, 2, ... 60
  • Column B (Beginning Lease Liability): Liability balance before that month's payment
  • Column C (Lease Payment): Fixed rent payment
  • Column D (Interest Expense): Financing charge for the period
  • Column E (Principal Reduction): Amount reducing the lease liability
  • Column F (Ending Lease Liability): Remaining obligation after payment
4 Input the Dynamic Row Formulas (For Advance Payments Type 1)

Because commercial leases are almost universally paid in advance (Month 1 payment made immediately on signing):

# Month 1 (Row 10): # Beginning Liability (Cell B10): =B6 (Links to PV result) # Payment (Cell C10): =$B$1 # Interest Expense (Cell D10): =0 (No interest accrued on Day 1 payment) # Principal Reduction (Cell E10): =C10 - D10 # Ending Liability (Cell F10): =B10 - E10 # Month 2 (Row 11): # Beginning Liability (Cell B11): =F10 # Interest Expense (Cell D11): =B11 * ($B$2 / 12) # Principal Reduction (Cell E11): =C11 - D11 # Ending Liability (Cell F11): =B11 - E11

Select cells B11:F11 and drag down through Month 60. At the final month, the ending liability in Column F will resolve cleanly to $0.00.

5 Add Right-of-Use (ROU) Asset Depreciation (ASC 842 Compliance)

Under ASC 842 for finance leases, straight-line depreciation of the ROU Asset is recognized separately:

# Monthly ROU Asset Amortization: =Initial_ROU_Asset / Total_Lease_Months

Total Monthly P&L Expense = Interest Expense (Column D) + Monthly ROU Amortization.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

To create a capital lease amortization schedule: 1) Discount future payments to present value using =PV(annual_rate/12, months, -payment, 0, type), 2) Set up columns for Beginning Liability, Payment, Interest, Principal Reduction, and Ending Liability, 3) For subsequent periods, calculate interest as Beginning Liability * (annual_rate/12), and 4) Subtract interest from payment to find principal reduction until the liability reaches zero.
Commercial real estate and equipment leases almost always require payments in advance (Annuity Due / Type 1). In Type 1, the first payment occurs immediately on Day 1, meaning zero interest has accrued and the entire first payment reduces principal. In Type 0 (arrears), the first payment occurs at month-end after 30 days of interest have accrued. Using the wrong type causes your balance sheet PV to be off by several thousand dollars.
The Incremental Borrowing Rate (IBR) is the rate of interest that a lessee would have to pay to borrow on a collateralized basis over a similar term an amount equal to the lease payments in a similar economic environment. If the rate implicit in the lease cannot be readily determined, ASC 842 requires the lessee to use their IBR to discount lease payments.
