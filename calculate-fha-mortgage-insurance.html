How to Calculate FHA Mortgage Insurance: 2026 HUD Guidelines

An FHA loan backed by the Federal Housing Administration is one of the most accessible mortgage programs in the United States, permitting down payments as low as 3.5% with credit scores starting at 580. However, in exchange for low down payment barriers and flexible debt-to-income limits, HUD requires all FHA borrowers to pay Mortgage Insurance Premiums (MIP).

1. The Two Components of FHA Mortgage Insurance

Unlike conventional loans that only charge monthly Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI), every FHA home purchase mandates two separate insurance premiums:

  • 1. Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (UFMIP): Mandated at a flat 1.75% of the base loan amount. If your base loan is $350,000, your UFMIP is $6,125. Over 99% of home buyers roll this fee into their loan balance rather than paying cash out of pocket at closing.
  • 2. Annual Mortgage Insurance Premium (Annual MIP): An ongoing percentage assessed on the base loan balance, paid in 12 equal monthly installments as part of your total mortgage payment (PITI + MIP).

Mathematical Formulas for FHA Mortgage Insurance

$$\text{Upfront UFMIP (\$)} = \text{Base Loan Amount} \times 0.0175$$ $$\text{Monthly MIP (\$)} = \frac{\text{Base Loan Amount} \times \text{Annual MIP Rate \%}}{12}$$

In March 2023, the White House and HUD instituted an across-the-board 30 basis point (0.30%) reduction in annual MIP fees. The table below outlines the current legal rate tiers across loan terms and down payments:

3. Can You Cancel FHA Mortgage Insurance? (11-Year Rule vs. Life of Loan)

One of the most frequent questions from home buyers is: "When can I drop FHA mortgage insurance?" Under HUD Mortgagee Letter rules instituted in 2013:

  • If you put down less than 10% (e.g. standard 3.5% down): You must pay monthly FHA MIP for the entire loan term (30 years). The only way to remove the monthly payment is to refinance into a Conventional mortgage once your property reaches 20% equity.
  • If you put down 10% or more: Your monthly MIP will automatically cancel after 11 years (132 monthly payments) without needing to refinance!

4. FHA MIP vs. Conventional PMI: Key Differences

Home buyers often compare FHA loans to Conventional 97 loans (3% down). While conventional private mortgage insurance (PMI) cancels automatically at 78% loan-to-value under the federal Homeowners Protection Act (HPA), FHA loans do not offer automatic 78% cancellation. However, FHA MIP has flat rates (0.55%) regardless of your credit score, making FHA significantly cheaper for borrowers with credit scores between 580 and 680.

Frequently Asked Questions (FHA MIP FAQs)

How do you calculate FHA mortgage insurance? ▼
FHA mortgage insurance has two distinct components: (1) Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium (UFMIP): Calculated as 1.75% of the base loan amount (e.g., $300,000 base loan × 1.75% = $5,250), which is typically financed into the loan balance; and (2) Annual Mortgage Insurance Premium (Annual MIP): For a standard 30-year loan with 3.5% down, the annual rate is 0.55% of the base loan amount. This annual amount is divided by 12 and added to your monthly mortgage payment ($300,000 × 0.55% ÷ 12 = $137.50/month).
What is the current FHA monthly MIP rate? ▼
Following HUD's 30 basis point reduction in 2023, the standard annual MIP for 30-year FHA loans with less than 5% down (such as the standard 3.5% down payment) is 0.55% (55 bps). If you put down 5% or more, the annual MIP drops to 0.50% (50 bps). For 15-year FHA loans with more than 10% down, the rate is as low as 0.15% (15 bps).
How long do you have to pay FHA mortgage insurance? ▼
The duration of FHA annual MIP depends strictly on your original down payment at closing: (1) If you put down less than 10% (LTV > 90%), you must pay monthly MIP for the entire life of the 30-year loan (it never cancels automatically); (2) If you put down 10% or more (LTV ≤ 90%), your monthly MIP automatically cancels after exactly 11 years (132 monthly payments).
Can FHA mortgage insurance (MIP) be removed without refinancing? ▼
If you put down less than 10% at purchase, FHA MIP cannot be removed without refinancing into a Conventional loan once you reach at least 20% home equity. If you put down 10% or more, it automatically drops off after 11 years without refinancing. Unlike conventional PMI, FHA MIP does not automatically cancel when you reach 78% or 80% equity.
Can the 1.75% Upfront MIP (UFMIP) be rolled into the mortgage? ▼
Yes, over 99% of FHA borrowers choose to finance the 1.75% Upfront Mortgage Insurance Premium directly into their total loan amount rather than paying it in cash at closing. For example, on a $400,000 home with 3.5% down ($14,000 down), the base loan is $386,000. Adding the 1.75% UFMIP ($6,755) results in a total financed loan balance of $392,755.
What is the difference between FHA MIP and Conventional PMI? ▼
FHA loans require both an upfront premium (1.75%) and monthly premiums (0.55%), and the monthly premium stays for the life of the loan for down payments under 10%. Conventional Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) has no upfront federal fee and legally must be automatically terminated under the Homeowners Protection Act once your loan balance reaches 78% of the original home value.
