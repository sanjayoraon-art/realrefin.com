VA Closing Cost Calculator: Complete 2026 Veteran Guide

Securing a home loan through the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is one of the most powerful financial benefits earned through military service. With 0% down payment requirements, competitive interest rates, and no ongoing monthly private mortgage insurance (PMI), VA mortgages offer unprecedented purchasing power. However, understanding your out-of-pocket settlement expenses requires knowing how the VA Funding Fee, VA non-allowable fees, and seller concessions interact.

1. 2026 VA Funding Fee Rates Chart

The VA Funding Fee is a one-time federal charge mandated by law to sustain the VA home loan guarantee program without burdening U.S. taxpayers. The statutory fee percentage is determined by whether it is your first-time use or subsequent use, and your down payment amount:

Down Payment Amount First-Time VA Loan Use Subsequent VA Loan Use
0% Down (Standard 100% LTV) 2.15% 3.30%
5.0% to 9.9% Down 1.50% 1.50%
10.0% or Greater Down 1.25% 1.25%
VA Disability (10%+ rating) 0.00% (100% Exempt) 0.00% (100% Exempt)

VA Funding Fee Mathematical Equation

$$\text{VA Funding Fee (\$)} = \text{Base Loan Amount} \times \text{Applicable Tier \%}$$ Note: Veterans almost universally choose to roll (finance) this fee directly into their total mortgage balance, meaning you do not need to bring this cash to the closing table.

2. 100% VA Funding Fee Exemption for Disabled Veterans (38 U.S.C. § 3729)

If you are a veteran receiving disability compensation from the VA for a service-connected disability rated at 10% or higher, you are legally exempt from paying the VA funding fee. This statutory waiver applies to:

  • Veterans receiving service-connected VA disability compensation (10% to 100% rating).
  • Veterans entitled to receive disability compensation who receive military retirement pay instead.
  • Active-duty service members who have been awarded the Purple Heart.
  • Surviving spouses receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC).

On a $450,000 purchase with 0% down, checking the disability exemption box on this calculator immediately saves you $9,675 (first use) or $14,850 (subsequent use).

3. VA Non-Allowable Fees: What Lenders Cannot Charge You

Under VA Lender Handbook Chapter 8, the VA establishes strict consumer protections regarding what fees a veteran borrower can and cannot be charged at closing. If a mortgage lender assesses a flat 1% origination fee, they are legally prohibited from unbundling and charging separate fees for:

  • Loan application or processing fees.
  • Document preparation or underwriting charges.
  • Lender legal fees or attorney document review fees.
  • Postage, courier, facsimile, or wire transfer fees.
  • Pest/termite inspections in certain states (must be paid by seller or lender).

4. The 4% VA Seller Concession Strategy

Under VA mortgage guidelines, the seller can contribute up to 4% of the reasonable property value in concessions. Unlike conventional loan credits (which can only cover actual closing costs), VA seller concessions can be utilized for:

  • Paying off the veteran's entire one-time VA funding fee.
  • Prepaying homeowners insurance and county property tax escrow accounts.
  • Purchasing discount points to buy down the veteran's permanent mortgage interest rate.
  • Paying off the veteran's preexisting consumer debts (such as auto loans or credit cards) at closing to help qualify for the loan.

5. Tennessee VA Closing Costs & Mortgage Recordation Taxes

In the State of Tennessee, closing costs include statutory excise and recordation taxes codified under Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-4-409:

Frequently Asked Questions (VA Closing Cost FAQs)

How much are closing costs on a VA loan, and what is the VA funding fee? ▼
VA loan closing costs typically range between 1% to 3% of the purchase price plus the one-time mandatory VA Funding Fee. For first-time VA loan borrowers with 0% down, the funding fee is 2.15% (reduced to 1.5% with 5% down and 1.25% with 10% down). For subsequent use, the fee is 3.3% with 0% down. Crucially, veterans with a service-connected disability rating of 10% or higher, active-duty Purple Heart recipients, and surviving spouses are 100% exempt from the VA funding fee ($0 fee).
Who is exempt from paying the VA funding fee? ▼
Under 38 U.S.C. § 3729, you are 100% exempt from paying the VA funding fee if you are: (1) A veteran receiving VA disability compensation for a service-connected disability of 10% or higher; (2) A veteran entitled to receive disability compensation who is instead receiving retirement pay; (3) An active-duty service member who has been awarded the Purple Heart; or (4) A surviving spouse of a veteran who died in service or from a service-connected disability.
Can I roll my VA closing costs and funding fee into the loan? ▼
The one-time VA funding fee can be 100% rolled into the mortgage loan balance on purchase transactions. Regular closing costs (lender fees, title, prepaids) cannot be rolled into a purchase loan; however, they can be covered completely by seller concessions (up to 4%), lender credits, or gift funds. On a VA IRRRL streamline refinance, all closing costs can be financed into the loan.
What are VA non-allowable closing fees? ▼
Under VA Lender Handbook Chapter 8, the VA explicitly forbids lenders from charging the veteran certain closing fees if the lender charges a 1% origination fee. Non-allowable fees include separate charges for loan application, document preparation, processing, underwriting, attorney fees for the lender, and courier fees.
Can the seller pay my VA loan closing costs? ▼
Yes! VA loan rules permit the seller to contribute up to 4% of the reasonable property value in seller concessions. These concessions can be used to pay the veteran's VA funding fee, discount points for an interest rate buydown, prepaid property taxes and insurance, or even pay off the veteran's existing consumer credit cards or auto loans at closing.
How do Tennessee (TN) closing costs apply to a VA loan? ▼
In Tennessee, real estate closing costs include two statutory state taxes under Tenn. Code Ann. § 67-4-409: (1) The Tennessee State Transfer Tax of $0.37 per $100 ($3.70 per $1,000) of value, customarily paid by the seller; and (2) The Tennessee Mortgage Recordation Tax of $0.115 per $100 ($1.15 per $1,000) on mortgage indebtedness exceeding $2,000 (first $2,000 exempt), customarily paid by the buyer.
