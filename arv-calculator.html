Comprehensive Guide: How to Calculate ARV in Real Estate & Wholesaling

In residential real estate investing, few metrics carry as much financial weight as **After Repair Value (ARV)**. Whether you are a real estate wholesaler calculating your contract assignment spread, a fix & flipper securing hard money loan leverage, or a BRRRR investor executing a cash-out refinance takeout, ARV represents the economic anchor of the entire transaction.

What is After Repair Value (ARV)?

After Repair Value (ARV) is the estimated fair market value of a distressed or dated residential property after all necessary repairs, capital improvements, cosmetic modernizations, and structural renovations have been completed.

The Mathematical Formula for Calculating ARV

Professional appraisers and institutional underwriters determine ARV using the **Sales Comparison Approach (Comps Method)**. The formula evaluates nearby recently sold renovated homes:

$$\text{Average Price per Sq Ft} = \frac{\sum (\text{Adjusted Comp Sale Price})}{\sum (\text{Comp Square Footage})}$$ $$\text{Subject Property ARV} = (\text{Subject Living Area Sq Ft} \times \text{Average Price per Sq Ft}) \pm \text{Net Subject Adjustments}$$

For example, if the average adjusted price per square foot of three nearby renovated comps is $215/sqft and your subject property has 1,850 square feet of finished living area, the baseline ARV is:

$$\text{ARV} = 1,850 \text{ sq ft} \times \$215.00/\text{sq ft} = \$397,750$$

How to Calculate ARV for Wholesaling (The Wholesaler's Spread)

Real estate wholesalers do not purchase homes to live in or renovate themselves. Instead, they secure a distressed property under contract with a motivated seller and assign that contractual right to a cash buyer (end investor) for a fee.

To prevent contracting an unsellable deal, wholesalers must reverse-engineer their purchase offer starting strictly from the ARV:

The Wholesaling ARV Formula:

$$\text{End-Buyer Purchase Price} = (\text{ARV} \times 70\%) - \text{Estimated Renovation Costs}$$ $$\text{Maximum Wholesaler Offer to Seller} = \text{End-Buyer Purchase Price} - \text{Target Assignment Fee}$$

Step-by-Step Wholesaling Example:

  1. Determine ARV: Neighborhood sold comps indicate a fully renovated value of $400,000.
  2. Estimate Rehab Costs: The property requires a moderate rehab ($45/sqft on 1,800 sqft) = $81,000.
  3. Apply the 70% Rule: $\$400,000 \times 0.70 = \$280,000$.
  4. Subtract Rehab: $\$280,000 - \$81,000 = \$199,000$ (This is the maximum your cash buyer will pay).
  5. Deduct Wholesaler Assignment Fee: If your target profit is $15,000, your maximum allowable offer to the motivated seller is: $$\$199,000 - \$15,000 = \$184,000$$

How to Find Accurate Real Estate Comps by Address

To calculate an accurate ARV by address, investors must follow strict appraisal comparability guidelines:

  • Proximity (Radius): Comps must be located within 0.5 miles for urban/suburban single-family homes, and within 1.0 mile for rural tracts. Never cross major geographic barriers such as interstate highways, railroad tracks, or school district boundaries.
  • Recency of Sale: Select properties that closed within the past 90 to 180 days. Older sales do not reflect current mortgage rate environments or seasonal buyer demand.
  • Square Footage Variance: Comps should be within $\pm 15\%$ of the subject property's finished above-grade gross living area (GLA).
  • Property Age & Style: Compare ranches to ranches and two-stories to two-stories, keeping the year built within a 10 to 15-year window.
  • Finished Renovation Condition: Ensure selected comps represent fully repaired, modernized homes with premium finishes (granite/quartz countertops, modern bathrooms, new HVAC, and updated flooring).
