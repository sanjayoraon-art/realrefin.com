How to Calculate Mortgage Refinance in Excel (Step-by-Step)

Whether you are building your own financial model in Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets, or using our pre-built downloadable template, mortgage refinance calculations rely on five fundamental financial equations.

1. Calculating Monthly Payment: The =PMT() Formula

Excel calculates amortized monthly payments using the standard annuity formula:

$$M = P \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}$$

In Excel, write:

# Excel Syntax: =PMT(annual_rate / 12, term_years * 12, -loan_principal) =PMT(B4/12, B5*12, -B3)

2. The Break-Even Recovery Period Formula

The break-even point is the exact duration in months before cumulative monthly interest savings repay your upfront loan origination and closing costs:

$$\text{Break-Even Months} = \frac{\text{Closing Costs}}{\text{Current Monthly Payment} - \text{New Monthly Payment}}$$
# Excel Formula: In cell B12 =B8 / (B6 - B7)

3. Calculating Maximum Cash-Out Equity (80% LTV Limit)

Lenders enforce maximum loan-to-value limits (typically 80% on conventional single-family homes). In Excel:

$$\text{Max Cash-Out} = (\text{Appraised Home Value} \times 0.80) - \text{Existing Loan Balance}$$
# Excel Formula: In cell B16 =MAX(0, (B14 * 0.80) - B3)

Institutional Guide: Rate-and-Term vs. Cash-Out Refinancing

Refinancing a residential mortgage is one of the highest-stakes financial decisions a property owner or real estate investor makes. While standard bank calculators solely highlight the headline monthly payment decrease, institutional underwriting requires examining closing cost friction, amortization timeline resets, and opportunity cost of capital.

1. When Does a Rate-and-Term Refinance Make Mathematical Sense?

Historically, mortgage professionals cited the "1% Rule"—advising borrowers to only refinance if the prevailing interest rate is at least 100 basis points (1.00%) below their current note rate. However, modern underwriting proves that loan balance heavily dictates viability:

  • Large Loan Balances ($500,000+): A drop of just 0.50% (50 bps) on a $600,000 loan reduces monthly payments by approximately $190/mo ($2,280/year). At a $4,500 closing cost, the break-even period is under 24 months, making the refinance exceptionally profitable.
  • Modest Loan Balances ($150,000 or less): Even a full 1.50% rate drop may only yield $110/mo in savings. If closing costs total $5,000, recouping costs takes over 45 months (nearly 4 years). If the homeowner relocates or sells before Year 4, they suffer a net financial loss.

2. Beware the "Amortization Reset Trap"

If you have already paid down 7 years of a 30-year mortgage, your monthly payments are significantly tilted toward principal paydown. If you refinance into a brand-new 30-year term to capture a lower monthly payment, you reset your amortization back to Year 1, where interest constitutes the vast majority of every payment. To counter this, savvy borrowers either:

  • Refinance into a 20-year or 15-year fixed loan to lock in lower rates while maintaining or accelerating equity build-up.
  • Keep paying their old higher monthly payment on the new lower-rate 30-year loan, directing 100% of the surplus toward monthly principal curtailment.

3. Cash-Out Refinance vs. Junior Liens (HELOC & Home Equity Loans)

If your primary mortgage rate was locked in between 2020 and 2021 at sub-3.5% historic lows, executing a full Cash-Out Refinance today forces you to surrender your ultra-low interest rate on your entire existing balance. In that scenario, retaining your first lien and taking out a secondary HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit) or Home Equity Loan delivers a drastically lower weighted blended interest rate.
