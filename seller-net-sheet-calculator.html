Seller Net Sheet Calculator: Complete Guide to Closing Costs & Proceeds

Before accepting an offer on your home, every seller needs to know their bottom-line walk-away figure. A **Seller Net Sheet** is the essential financial document prepared by listing agents, closing attorneys, and title companies that itemizes every single dollar deducted from your gross contract price at the closing table.

1. How to Calculate Seller Net Proceeds

The core mathematical formula governing any real estate seller net sheet is:

Seller Net Proceeds Formula

$$\text{Net Proceeds} = \text{Contract Sale Price} - \text{Mortgage Payoffs} - \text{Commissions} - \text{Title \& Escrow} - \text{Transfer Taxes} - \text{Prorations \& Credits}$$

2. State-Specific Closing Rules: Florida, Texas & California

Closing costs for home sellers vary dramatically depending on the state where the real property is located:

3. Understanding the Florida Seller Net Sheet: Doc Stamps on Deed

In Florida, the single largest closing fee behind real estate commissions is the **Documentary Stamp Tax on Deeds** (Florida Statute § 201.02).

  • In 66 out of 67 Florida counties, the tax is calculated as $0.70 per $100 of consideration. For instance, on a $500,000 home sale, the state doc stamp fee is exactly $\$500,000 \times 0.007 = \$3,500$.
  • In Miami-Dade County, the tax is $0.60 per $100, plus an additional $0.45 surtax on properties that are not single-family residences.

4. The Texas Seller Net Sheet: Zero Transfer Tax & TDI Charts

Texas is one of the most seller-friendly states in the nation regarding transactional taxes. The State of Texas constitutionally prohibits transfer taxes on real estate deeds.

However, Texas regulates title insurance premiums under the **Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) Title Insurance Basic Premium Rates**. Under Texas law, title companies cannot discount or compete on title insurance rates; every title insurer must charge the exact statutory rate on the promulgated chart (e.g., $2,583 on a $450,000 home).

5. Post-NAR Settlement Commission Structures

Under post-2024 NAR antitrust settlement rules:

  1. The seller negotiates their listing agent's commission independently (e.g., 2% to 3%).
  2. Buyer agent commissions are no longer published on MLS platforms. Buyers negotiate their representation fee directly with their agent.
  3. Sellers may offer a **Buyer Broker Concession** at closing (e.g., 2% to 2.5%) to make their listing attractive to qualified purchasers who may lack liquid cash to pay their agent out-of-pocket.

Frequently Asked Questions: Seller Net Sheets

What is a seller net sheet in real estate? +
A seller net sheet is a closing financial projection that calculates the exact amount of cash a homeowner will receive when selling their home. It starts with the agreed-upon sales price and itemizes all deductions including existing mortgage payoffs, brokerage fees, title insurance, transfer taxes, escrow fees, and property tax prorations.
How does the Florida seller net sheet calculate Doc Stamps? +
In Florida, transfer taxes are called Documentary Stamp Taxes on the Deed. State law sets the rate at 70 cents per $100 of the total purchase price (0.70%) in all counties except Miami-Dade. For example, selling a home for $400,000 results in $2,800 in Florida doc stamps paid by the seller at closing.
Why is a Texas seller net sheet different from other states? +
Texas has zero transfer taxes on real estate sales, saving sellers thousands. However, title insurance rates in Texas are set by state statute through the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI), meaning all title agencies charge identical, non-negotiable insurance premiums based on the purchase price.
What are customary seller closing costs in California? +
California sellers customarily pay county documentary transfer taxes ($1.10 per $1,000 of consideration, or 0.11%), municipal city transfer taxes (where applicable), half of the escrow company fee, an owner's title policy (in Southern California), a Natural Hazard Disclosure (NHD) report (~$120), and any negotiated buyer closing cost credits.
How do property tax prorations affect my net proceeds? +
Because property taxes are paid in arrears in most states, the seller owes taxes for the portion of the year they owned the property before the closing date. At closing, the title company calculates the exact per-diem tax amount and deducts it from the seller's proceeds, crediting it to the buyer who will pay the full tax bill at year-end.
Can I negotiate seller closing costs? +
Yes. While statutory government transfer taxes (like Florida doc stamps or California documentary taxes) are fixed by law, other fees—such as real estate commissions, buyer closing credits, home warranties, and escrow fees—are fully negotiable in your purchase contract.
